A closed-loop system that pays for itself

Aquaculture facilities generate enormous volumes of organic residuals—mortalities, processing offcuts, uneaten feed, and biosolids. BSFL bioconversion transforms these liabilities into revenue-generating products that aquaculture operations already need.

Three byproducts. Three revenue paths.

●

Live BSFL

High-protein live feed

Fresh-harvested larvae are a nutrient-dense, high-fat, high-protein live feed for finfish, shrimp, and broodstock. They stimulate natural feeding behavior, improve growth rates, and reduce dependence on wild-caught fishmeal. Live BSFL can be sized and staged for direct delivery to ponds, tanks, and hatcheries.

●

Dried BSFL

Shelf-stable protein ingredient

Dried larvae offer a storable, transportable, high-protein ingredient for aquafeed formulation. With roughly 40% protein and 30% lipid content, dried BSFL replace fishmeal in compound feeds, reducing raw material costs and supply chain volatility. They integrate directly into existing feed-mill processes.

●

Frass

Nutrient-rich organic amendment

BSFL frass—the residual after bioconversion—is a balanced, slow-release organic fertilizer rich in nitrogen, phosphorus, and chitin. In aquaculture, frass drives beneficial microbial activity in biofloc and periphyton systems, improves pond productivity, and supports integrated multi-trophic operations.

Why this opportunity is extraordinary

Eliminate waste disposal costs

Aquaculture organic residuals are expensive to haul and permitted to dispose of. BSFL consume them on-site, converting a cost center into a production input.

Replace fishmeal with on-site protein

Global fishmeal prices are volatile and supply-constrained. BSFL grown on your own waste stream produce a consistent, locally sourced protein and lipid replacement.

Close the nutrient loop

Nitrogen and phosphorus that would leave your facility as waste instead cycle back through frass and larvae, keeping nutrients productive within the aquaculture system.

Stack multiple revenue streams

Every batch produces saleable outputs: live larvae for direct feeding, dried larvae for feed markets, and frass for agricultural and aquaculture soil markets. The economics compound.
